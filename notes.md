# Wind-inclusive January infeasibility. 
Under the deadline calibrated for the wave-only model (8% buffer over service-speed transit time), the wind-inclusive DP optimizer returns infeasible for January across the full plausible calibration range of the windage coefficient (target R_added/R_calm ratio 0.15–0.25 at the Hs=3m/Tp=9s/head/service-speed reference point). July remains feasible at every calibration tested.

A bisection search on the deadline shows the infeasibility is not severe: January requires only 1.7–5.0% additional schedule slack beyond the wave-only buffer, scaling monotonically with the windage calibration (Table X). At the minimum feasible deadline, wind-inclusive fuel consumption is 444.6–468.2 t, versus 405.2 t for the wave-only golden reference — an increase of 9.7–15.6% attributable to properly modeling wind resistance, once the schedule is loosened enough to make the comparison meaningful.

The overrun is not uniform across the route. Legs 1–6 account for the entire deadline shortfall, and per-leg overrun correlates strongly with the ERA5 wind-direction-variance flag (Pearson r = 0.70–0.75 across all three calibrations tested), while legs with stable wind direction show near-zero or negative overrun regardless of wind speed. This suggests the infeasibility is driven less by absolute wind strength than by directional inconsistency within the ERA5 averaging window — though whether this reflects genuine meteorological variability along this stretch of the January North Atlantic route, or an artifact of the spatial/temporal coarseness of the ERA5 extraction, cannot be distinguished with the current data granularity and is left as a limitation.

Conclusion. We report the January wind-inclusive infeasibility as a genuine finding rather than resolving it via deadline adjustment or windage recalibration: it indicates that the 3.4% fuel-savings figure reported for the wave-only model understates the true cost of the January transit, and that voyage planning under this route/season combination is materially more schedule-constrained once wind is correctly accounted for.


# Wind-inclusive results. 
Under the deadline calibrated for the wave-only model (8% buffer over service-speed transit time, 202.47h), the wind-inclusive DP optimizer is infeasible for January at the production windage calibration (target R_added/R_calm ratio 0.20 at Hs=3m/Tp=9s/head/service-speed). A bisection search on the deadline shows this infeasibility is not severe in absolute terms: January requires 1.7–5.0% additional schedule slack beyond the wave-only buffer, scaling monotonically with the windage calibration tested (ratio 0.15→209.64h → ratio 0.25). July remains feasible at every calibration tested and at the standard deadline.

We report the infeasibility itself as a finding (Section 4.9, per the project plan) rather than resolving it via ad hoc deadline extension. To characterize the optimizer's behavior once a minimum feasible schedule is granted, we reran the January prediction at 209.64h — the minimum feasible deadline at the production calibration (ratio 0.20) — and persisted both wind-inclusive results to the prediction database for direct comparison:

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| January (wind-inclusive) | 209.64h (loosened) | 454.0 t | 456.1 t | 0.5% |
| July (wind-inclusive) | 202.47h (standard) | 372.3 t | 384.3 t | 3.1% |


Two results follow. First, wind-inclusive fuel consumption in January (454.0 t) is markedly higher than the wave-only golden reference (405.2 t) — a ~12% increase attributable to properly modeling wind resistance, consistent with the pattern found in the calibration sweep (444.6–468.2 t across the tested ratio range). Second, and more notably, the DP optimizer's fuel advantage over the constant-RPM baseline collapses under harsh wind conditions: January's savings drop from the wave-only figure of 3.4% to essentially zero (0.5%) once wind is included and the deadline is loosened just enough for feasibility, while July's savings (3.1%) remain close to its wave-only figure (3.7%). We attribute this to the schedule constraint binding across most legs under harsh wind — with the earlier bisection showing the deadline shortfall concentrated on legs 1–6, whose overrun correlates strongly with the ERA5 wind-direction-variance flag (Pearson r = 0.70–0.75 across calibrations) rather than with wind speed alone — leaving the optimizer little room to redistribute fuel-expensive transit time onto more favorable legs. In effect, the value of RPM optimization is itself weather-dependent, and diminishes precisely under the harsh, schedule-tight conditions where fuel savings would matter most.

A caveat: the January and July wind-inclusive figures above use different deadlines (209.64h vs 202.47h respectively) and are therefore not directly comparable to each other or to the wave-only golden reference on an apples-to-apples schedule basis. The January figure represents the best achievable outcome given the minimum schedule extension required for feasibility, not a like-for-like comparison at the original planning deadline.
